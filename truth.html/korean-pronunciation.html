The “N-word” sound-alike claim

Korean lyrics, English listeners, and what the available evidence supports

What listeners noticed

Some English-speaking listeners have said that Korean words in songs—often written in romanization as “niga” or “naega”—sound like an English racial slur to them. Romanized Korean can be unfamiliar to listeners, and a perceived sound-alike can understandably prompt questions or discomfort.

In Korean lyrics, these are Korean pronoun forms, not the English slur. Similarity in sound to some listeners does not make the words equivalent in meaning, and the pronunciation or spelling in romanization should not be treated as evidence that the English slur was used.

“Fake Love” and lyric-change claims

On May 24, 2018, BTS held a press conference in Seoul for their album Love Yourself: Tear. Reporters discussed the group’s performance of “Fake Love” at the Billboard Music Awards earlier that week, where Suga and J-Hope performed altered versions of lines containing the Korean pronouns “naega” and “niga.”

RM explained that first-time English-speaking listeners might misunderstand those sounds, so the lyrics were adjusted for the performance while preserving the sound of the song. In this context, “naega” means “I” and “niga” means “you” in Korean; the words were not the English racial slur.

Press conference event details

  • Date and location: May 24, 2018, Seoul, South Korea.
  • Event: Press conference for Love Yourself: Tear.
  • Performance discussed: BTS’s “Fake Love” performance at the 2018 Billboard Music Awards.
  • Explanation: RM said the altered Korean lyrics were intended to avoid possible misunderstanding by listeners hearing the song for the first time in English.
