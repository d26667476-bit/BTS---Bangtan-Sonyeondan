Misogynistic lyrics and the response

Context on the 2015–2016 criticism, the agency’s apology, and RM’s reflection

What was criticized?

Fans and listeners criticized some early BTS lyrics and social media posts as misogynistic or objectifying. Reporting at the time specifically discussed RM’s solo track “Joke” and described the controversy as involving the group’s lyrics and tweets. “War of Hormone” and “Converse High” were also among the songs discussed by critics.

The criticism was about the words and perspectives in the material—not proof of a single interpretation of every song or of the members’ private beliefs.

Big Hit’s 2016 apology

In July 2016, Big Hit Entertainment publicly apologized to people offended by controversial lyrics and social media posts. Its statement acknowledged that the company was not free of social prejudice and that defining women’s roles and values from a male perspective was wrong. The agency said it would take the criticism into account in future work.

“We realize that we are not yet free from social prejudices and mistakes and that it is wrong to define women’s roles and values from the men’s perspective.”

Big Hit Entertainment statement, quoted by The Korea Times, July 8, 2016.

RM’s reflection

RM later reflected on the effect his words and actions could have, even when harm was not intended, and on the need to take responsibility and change.
