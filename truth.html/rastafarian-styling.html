Rastafarian imagery and styling

Fan criticism of J-Hope’s 2019 “Chicken Noodle Soup” promotional look

What prompted the criticism?

In September 2019, before the release of J-Hope and Becky G’s “Chicken Noodle Soup” video, promotional images showed J-Hope with hair styled in thick twists. Some fans said the look resembled locs and criticized the use of a Black hairstyle as part of a hip-hop visual concept. Other fans disputed that interpretation, describing the style as gel twists. Teen Vogue reported on the debate and noted that the promotional styling was also used in connection with the track’s promotion.

Some fans also objected to promotional artwork they read as combining loc-like styling with red, gold, and green colors associated with Rastafari. That specific interpretation appears in the fan petition linked below; it should be understood as the critics’ reading of the imagery, not as a statement confirmed by the contemporary Teen Vogue report.

Reported changes and what is documented

The 2019 fan petition asked Big Hit and J-Hope for an apology and said the label continued promoting the song without acknowledging the concerns at that time. This records the petitioners’ experience and request; it is not an official agency response.

Some fans later reported that the cartoon promotional image was no longer used in certain digital thumbnails. Teen Vogue documented the cartoon image and the backlash, but the sources cited here do not independently confirm when or why a thumbnail changed. The alleged removal should therefore be treated as fan-reported, not as a verified agency action caused by the campaign.

Fans have also pointed to later live performances, including BTS’s 2021 Muster Sowoozoo, as examples of different styling for the song. The sources linked here do not establish that a particular costume change was made in response to the criticism. Nor do they substantiate claims that Big Hit adopted a company-wide ban or formal styling policy after 2019.

One documented aspect of the song’s cultural context is its musical source: J-Hope and Becky G described their 2019 “Chicken Noodle Soup” as a tribute to the 2006 song by Webstar and Young B, featuring AG. The collaboration and credited homage are relevant context, but do not by themselves resolve the styling concerns.

Cultural context and limits

For critics, the concern was that a hairstyle associated with Black identity and a visual treatment interpreted as Rastafarian symbolism were being used as aesthetic shorthand for a hip-hop concept. Others disagreed about the hairstyle itself. The available sources document the disagreement and the criticism; they do not establish the creators’ intent, a formal agency response, or a later internal styling policy.

This entry describes a cultural-appropriation controversy, not a finding that a particular individual intended to express anti-Black or anti-Rastafarian views.
