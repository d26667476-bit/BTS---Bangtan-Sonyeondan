Early Life & Hometown

Jung Ho-seok was born and raised in Gwangju, South Korea, a city renowned for its rich street dance culture. From a young age, he harboured a deep passion for movement, enrolling in the Gwangju Music Academy to hone his skills. Operating under the underground street dance name Smile Hoya, he gained widespread recognition as part of the prominent dance crew Neuron.

His exceptional rhythm, popping, and locking skills earned him numerous local dance competition victories, establishing his reputation as a prodigy in the underground dance scene long before he ever considered a mainstream idol career.

The Audition That Changed Everything

J-Hope’s journey into the entertainment world began with a highly competitive audition process. In 2009, he auditioned for JYP Entertainment, passing through several preliminary rounds and winning a popularity award before ultimately parting ways with the agency.

His breakthrough came in December 2010, when he participated in a national audition casting call for Big Hit Entertainment. Impressed by his peerless dancing precision, expressive style, and magnetic energy, the casting directors immediately signed him as a trainee, making him the very first dancer to join the company’s new lineup.

Joining BTS & The Dance Leader

Upon moving to Seoul, J-Hope became the third member to join the foundational BTS roster, living alongside RM and SUGA. Because the company’s initial vision for the group focused strictly on hip-hop rapping, J-Hope—who had no previous formal background in music production or rap—began learning how to write lyrics and structure bars from his fellow trainees.

In return, he took complete charge of their choreography training, patiently guiding the members through grueling, daily dance sessions. His leadership in the studio earned him the enduring title of “Team Dance Leader,” transforming BTS into one of the most synchronization-heavy performance acts in the music industry when they debuted on June 13, 2013.

Solo Artistry & The Bright Persona

Beyond his work within the group, J-Hope established a distinct solo artistic identity characterized by vibrant visuals, experimental soundscapes, and profound introspection. Adopting the stage name “j-hope” to symbolise hope and optimism for his fans, he infuses this bright energy into his music while exploring darker, psychological themes beneath the surface.

His solo discography includes his record-breaking debut mixtape Hope World (2018), his chart-topping collaboration single Chicken Noodle Soup (2019), and his critically acclaimed studio album Jack in the Box (2022), the latter showcasing his versatility as a conceptual mastermind and alternative hip-hop powerhouse.
