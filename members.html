Early Life & Hometown

Kim Seok-jin was born in Anyang but moved to the peaceful city of Gwacheon, Gyeonggi Province, when he was just a baby, where he spent his formative years. Growing up in a supportive, well-off family, Jin was initially quite introverted and didn’t have immediate aspirations toward music. In middle school, he even went abroad to Australia as an exchange student.

His life's trajectory shifted in high school after watching the historical K-drama Queen Seondeok, which inspired him to pursue acting professionally so he could move people with his emotions. Keeping true to this dream, he beat immense competitive odds (a 1:210 acceptance ratio) to enrol at Konkuk University in Seoul, majoring in Theater and Film Studies.

The Audition That Changed Everything

Long before joining Big Hit Entertainment, Jin’s striking visuals caught the eye of a talent scout from SM Entertainment during his junior high school days. However, assuming it was a fraudulent scam, he famously ran away and rejected the offer.

His real breakthrough arrived during his early college years. While stepping off a public bus on his way to his acting classes at university, a casting director from Big Hit Entertainment spotted him simply because of his incredible presence and symmetry. He was invited to a private audition, where he originally tried out strictly as an actor rather than a pop idol, having zero formal background in singing or dancing.

Joining BTS & Overcoming the Odds

Big Hit staff recognized his star potential and guided him toward becoming an idol trainee instead. He officially signed his contract in 2011, making him the fourth member to join the group’s foundational lineup. Coming from a background entirely centered around acting, Jin had to start completely from scratch, spending countless hours practicing vocal runs and hip-hop choreography.

He balanced this grueling three-year training regimen while physically attending his university classes in person. He was the final member to officially move into the trainee dorms in July 2012, ultimately cementing his spot as the core visual and emotional sub-vocalist for BTS's historic debut on June 13, 2013.

Early Life & Hometown

Min Yoon-gi was born and raised in the Buk District of Daegu, South Korea. He discovered his passion for hip-hop at a young age after hearing Epik High’s Fly, which inspired him to start writing lyrics by the age of 13. By 17, he was working a part-time job at a recording studio in Daegu to learn music production.

Operating under the underground rap name Gloss, he became an established producer and rapper in the local underground hip-hop scene, composing beats and arranging music even while facing financial hardships and a lack of support for his musical ambitions.

The Audition That Changed Everything

SUGA’s path to Big Hit Entertainment wasn’t a traditional idol audition. In 2010, he saw a flyer for Big Hit’s first “Hit It” rap audition championship. He didn’t audition to be an idol performer; his goal was strictly to join the company as a music producer and songwriter.

He entered the competition and successfully won second place in the final rap battle showcase. His sharp lyricism, unique rhythmic flow, and advanced music production skills immediately caught the attention of company founder Bang Si-hyuk.

Joining BTS & The Producer Trap

After passing the audition in November 2010, SUGA moved from Daegu to Seoul as the second member to join the foundational BTS lineup, right after RM. Famously, Bang Si-hyuk initially convinced SUGA to join the group by promising him that he wouldn’t have to dance much and could focus primarily on producing music. Instead, he was placed into an intensive idol trainee regimen.

To make ends meet during his grueling three-year training period, SUGA worked a part-time job as a delivery driver, during which he suffered a severe shoulder injury that he hid from the company out of fear of being dropped. His perseverance paid off, and he officially debuted as BTS’s lead rapper and co-producer on June 13, 2013.

Solo Artistry & The Alter Ego: Agust D

Beyond his work with BTS, SUGA unleashes a raw, unvarnished underground rap persona through his solo alter ego, Agust D. The name itself is deeply tied to his roots: it spells “DT Suga” backwards, which stands for “Daegu Town Suga.” Under this moniker, he releases raw, self-produced projects that bypass traditional commercial boundaries to address heavy topics like mental health, depression, anxiety, social commentary, and his personal rise from poverty to global fame.

His trilogy of projects—the self-titled mixtape Agust D (2016), D-2 (2020) featuring the global hit Daechwita, and his official studio album D-DAY (2023)—fully solidified his reputation as an elite lyricist, capable of balancing mainstream pop appeal with the aggressive, fast-paced tongue technology of underground hip-hop.

Early Life & Hometown

Jung Ho-seok was born and raised in Gwangju, South Korea, a city renowned for its rich street dance culture. From a young age, he harboured a deep passion for movement, enrolling in the Gwangju Music Academy to hone his skills. Operating under the underground street dance name Smile Hoya, he gained widespread recognition as part of the prominent dance crew Neuron.

His exceptional rhythm, popping, and locking skills earned him numerous local dance competition victories, establishing his reputation as a prodigy in the underground dance scene long before he ever considered a mainstream idol career.

The Audition That Changed Everything

J-Hope’s journey into the entertainment world began with a highly competitive audition process. In 2009, he auditioned for JYP Entertainment, passing through several preliminary rounds and winning a popularity award before ultimately parting ways with the agency.

His breakthrough came in December 2010, when he participated in a national audition casting call for Big Hit Entertainment. Impressed by his peerless dancing precision, expressive style, and magnetic energy, the casting directors immediately signed him as a trainee, making him the very first dancer to join the company’s new lineup.

Joining BTS & The Dance Leader

Upon moving to Seoul, J-Hope became the third member to join the foundational BTS roster, living alongside RM and SUGA. Because the company’s initial vision for the group focused strictly on hip-hop rapping, J-Hope—who had no previous formal background in music production or rap—began learning how to write lyrics and structure bars from his fellow trainees.

In return, he took complete charge of their choreography training, patiently guiding the members through grueling, daily dance sessions. His leadership in the studio earned him the enduring title of “Team Dance Leader,” transforming BTS into one of the most synchronization-heavy performance acts in the music industry when they debuted on June 13, 2013.

Solo Artistry & The Bright Persona

Beyond his work within the group, J-Hope established a distinct solo artistic identity characterized by vibrant visuals, experimental soundscapes, and profound introspection. Adopting the stage name “j-hope” to symbolise hope and optimism for his fans, he infuses this bright energy into his music while exploring darker, psychological themes beneath the surface.

His solo discography includes his record-breaking debut mixtape Hope World (2018), his chart-topping collaboration single Chicken Noodle Soup (2019), and his critically acclaimed studio album Jack in the Box (2022), the latter showcasing his versatility as a conceptual mastermind and alternative hip-hop powerhouse.

Early Life & Hometown

Kim Nam-joon was born in Dongjak District, Seoul, but moved to Ilsan, Goyang, when he was four years old, where he spent his entire childhood. An exceptionally gifted student, he taught himself fluent English by watching the American sitcom Friends and scored in the top 1% of the nation in his high school university entrance exams, boasting an IQ of 148.

He discovered hip-hop in the fifth grade after hearing Epik High’s Fly and began writing lyrics shortly after. Operating under the underground hip-hop name Runch Randa, he quickly built a reputation in the underground rap scene, collaborating with other underground artists and showcasing a lyrical maturity far beyond his years.

The Audition That Changed Everything

RM’s path to global fame began through an underground hip-hop network. In 2009, he auditioned for the independent hip-hop label Big Deal Records, making it past the first round but missing out on the final selection. However, his extraordinary wordplay caught the eye of Korean rapper Sleepy, who was acting as a judge.

Deeply impressed by RM’s potential, Sleepy passed his contact information directly to Bang Si-hyuk, the founder of Big Hit Entertainment. In 2010, RM formally auditioned for Bang Si-hyuk, who was so blown away by the young teenager’s raw poetry and rhythm that he decided to form a hip-hop group entirely centered around him.

Joining BTS & The Foundation of the Group

RM was the very first member to join Big Hit’s new project in 2010, making him the foundational pillar of BTS. The entire group was built around his talent, and the lineup shifted multiple times from a pure hip-hop crew into a mainstream pop-idol group.

For three years, RM trained intensely while acting as the primary anchor for the incoming members, guiding them through the transition to Seoul life. Despite being younger than Jin and SUGA, his incredible maturity, creative vision, and English fluency led him to be appointed the official Leader of BTS, a role he assumed ahead of their official debut on June 13, 2013.

Solo Artistry & The Creative Mind

Beyond his monumental duties as the leader and primary lyricist for BTS, RM has established himself as a highly respected solo artist, poet, and art connoisseur. His solo work reflects deep philosophical introspection, exploring themes of identity, fame, and human connection.

He has released two monumental mixtapes—the aggressive, self-titled RM (2015) and the atmospheric, universally praised mono. (2018). He made his official solo commercial debut with the studio album Indigo (2022), followed by the critically acclaimed, genre-bending experimental album Right Place, Wrong Person (2024), solidifying his rank as one of the most prolific and profound songwriters of his generation.

Early Life & Hometown

Born and raised in the Geumjeong District of Busan, South Korea, Jimin discovered his passion for physical expression and performance at an early age. Growing up, he trained in martial arts, earning a black belt in Taekwondo and practicing Kendo for nearly eight years. As a teenager, he fell in love with dance and entered the prestigious Busan High School of Arts, where he became the top overall student in the modern dance department.

The Audition That Changed Everything

Encouraged by his contemporary dance teacher, Jimin auditioned for Big Hit Entertainment in a private regional casting session in Busan in 2012. His expressive movement and vocal potential helped him stand out, and he was the only dancer to pass that regional audition before moving to Seoul to begin training.

Joining BTS & The Shortest Training Period

Jimin trained for roughly six months to a year, the shortest training period among BTS members. He adapted from contemporary modern dance to fast-paced hip-hop choreography through intense practice, securing his place as the final member added to the group's debut lineup. He officially debuted with BTS on 13 June 2013.

Solo Artistry & Vulnerable Vocal Power

Jimin's distinctive falsetto and emotionally expressive performance style shine in BTS solo tracks such as Lie, Serendipity, and Filter. His solo album FACE (2023) featured Like Crazy, which reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100. He followed it with MUSE (2024), further exploring his identity as a solo artist.

Early Life & Hometown

Kim Tae-hyung was born in the Seo District of Daegu, South Korea, and spent his formative childhood years being raised by his grandparents in the rural countryside of Geochang County. Growing up in a farming family, he developed a love of music in middle school. With his father's support, he studied saxophone for three years, building musical foundations as he dreamed of becoming a professional performer.

The Audition That Changed Everything

In 2011, V went to a private Big Hit casting session in Daegu strictly to support a friend. A rookie development executive noticed his visuals and charisma and urged him to audition too. After calling his parents for permission, V auditioned on the spot and passed the casting session.

Joining BTS & The “Secret Member”

V signed with Big Hit in September 2011 and moved to Seoul as a trainee. The company kept him out of public pre-debut promotions as a “Secret Member”, then revealed him to the world 11 days before BTS's official debut on 13 June 2013.

Solo Artistry & Jazz Infusions

V's deep baritone and interest in vintage jazz, soul, and R&B shape a distinct solo style. BTS solo tracks such as Stigma, Singularity, and Inner Child showcase his expressive performances. He appeared in the historical drama Hwarang: The Poet Warrior Youth (2016) and released his debut solo studio album, Layover, in 2023, blending low-fi pop with classic jazz arrangements.

Early Life & Hometown

Jeon Jung-kook was born and raised in Mandeok-dong, Busan, South Korea. He enjoyed sports as a child and initially dreamed of becoming a professional badminton player. After seeing a performance by G-Dragon, he became interested in pursuing music and performance.

The Audition That Changed Everything

In 2011, at 13 years old, Jungkook travelled to Daegu to audition for the South Korean talent show Superstar K3. Although he did not advance from the preliminary rounds, industry scouts noticed his potential, and seven entertainment agencies approached him with offers.

Joining BTS & The Golden Choice

Despite offers from several established labels, Jungkook chose Big Hit Entertainment because he was impressed by RM's rapping and wanted to train with him. He moved to Seoul as a trainee and travelled to Los Angeles in 2012 for dance training at Movement Lifestyle. His skills across vocals, dance, and rap earned him the nickname “Golden Maknae”. He debuted with BTS on 13 June 2013.

Solo Artistry & Global Pop Domination

Jungkook's BTS solo tracks include Begin, Euphoria, and My Time. In 2022, he released Dreamers for the FIFA World Cup. His 2023 solo single Seven (featuring Latto) debuted at number one on the Billboard Hot 100, followed by his chart-topping debut solo album GOLDEN.
